## Review Rules

1. Do NOT rewrite the survey yourself.
2. Identify issues and provide specific, actionable modification instructions.
3. Prioritize issues: 馃敶 CRITICAL > 馃煛 MODERATE > 馃數 NICE-TO-HAVE.
4. **Depth Assessment**: For each core section (2-5), evaluate whether it has sufficient analytical depth or merely lists papers. Flag sections that are too shallow.
5. **Cross-Domain Coverage Check**: Scan the draft for any content that connects the core topic to adjacent disciplines or traditions. Check for:
   - Connection to systematic review methodology (PRISMA, screening, evidence synthesis)
   - Connection to scientometrics / science of science / citation analysis
   - Connection to evaluation standards from adjacent fields (summarization evaluation, factual consistency metrics)
   - Connection to non-CS domains (biomedical, social sciences) if relevant
   - If none of these are present in any section, flag as a CRITICAL gap: "The survey lacks cross-domain context. The Generator should add at least one paragraph or subsection connecting the core topic to an adjacent disciplinary perspective, such as systematic review standards, scientometrics, or relevant evaluation traditions."
   - If only one is present but superficially, flag as a MODERATE gap with specific recommendations for deepening the connection.

6. **Claim Boundary Scan**: Scan the draft for universal quantifiers ("all", "no", "every", "never",
   "universally", "the field"). For each occurrence, verify that:
   - The claim is explicitly scoped to the methods/benchmarks the survey examined
   - Counter-examples within the survey's own coverage are not contradicting the claim
   - The scope is restated in the same sentence or the immediately preceding sentence
   If any universal claim fails these checks, flag it as CRITICAL with the specific sentence
   and the corrective formulation.

7. **Self-Contradiction Check**: Cross-reference universal claims across sections:
   - If Section 4 says "no method does X" but Section 5 discusses a method that does X,
     flag as CRITICAL contradiction
   - If a strong claim in Section 6 is only weakly supported by the method descriptions in
     Sections 3-4, flag as MODERATE overgeneralization

## Focus Section Identification (Rounds 2+)

After reviewing all sections, identify the **2-3 sections that most need depth improvement**. For each focus section, diagnose the **specific knowledge gap** 鈥?what is missing that, if added, would most improve the analysis. Then formulate **search keywords** the Generator can use to find new papers addressing that gap. Write to `memory/focus_sections.md` in this format:
```yaml
focus_sections:
  - section: "Section 3: Citation Graph Expansion Strategies"
    issue: "Comparison of keyword-based vs embedding-based retrieval lacks depth 鈥?no analysis of trade-offs between precision and recall"
    knowledge_gap: "Quantitative comparison of retrieval strategies for citation graph expansion"
    search_keywords: ["citation graph retrieval precision recall", "embedding vs keyword academic paper search"]
  - section: "Section 4: Planning and Retrieval"
    issue: "No discussion of hierarchical planning vs flat retrieval 鈥?these are fundamentally different paradigms"
    knowledge_gap: "Hierarchical planning vs flat retrieval paradigms in survey generation"
    search_keywords: ["hierarchical survey planning", "flat retrieval vs structured planning survey generation"]
  - section: "Section 7: Evaluation Benchmarks"
    issue: "Evaluation comparability crisis mentioned but no supporting evidence from multiple benchmarks"
    knowledge_gap: "Cross-benchmark comparison of survey evaluation metrics (Rouge, BERTScore, human evaluation)"
    search_keywords: ["survey evaluation benchmark comparison", "Rouge vs BERTScore human evaluation correlation"]
criteria: "These sections were identified as the weakest in terms of analytical depth: they currently list methods without comparison tables, lack pros/cons analysis, or have insufficient mechanism-level detail."
```

### Knowledge Gap Formulation Guidelines

- A good knowledge_gap is **specific** 鈥?not "need more papers" but "need papers comparing X and Y on dimension Z"
- A good search_keyword is **targeted** 鈥?2-4 keywords that, when searched on arXiv, will surface relevant new papers
- If the focus section already has enough papers but lacks analysis, the knowledge_gap should target **comparative evidence** (e.g., "benchmark results of methods A vs B on task C")
- If the focus section is missing an entire sub-topic, the knowledge_gap should target that sub-topic directly

Selection criteria for focus sections:
- Sections that are mostly "paper lists" without comparative analysis
- Sections missing comparison tables where 3+ methods exist
- Sections without mechanism-level explanations
- Sections shorter than expected for their content density

## Evidence Retrieval (Rounds 2+)

After identifying focus sections and their knowledge gaps, retrieve new papers to fill those gaps. This ensures the Generator receives not just instructions but also the evidence it needs.

### When to Retrieve
Only when `search_keywords` were formulated for a focus section. Do NOT retrieve if focus sections only need restructuring (not new evidence).

### Retrieval Protocol
For each focus section with `search_keywords`:

**Step 1: Search**
- Use `arxiv_search` with each keyword query, max_results=10.
- Read abstracts. Select the 1-3 papers most relevant to the `knowledge_gap`.
- Skip papers already in `phase0/02_candidate_pool.md` (check by title or arXiv ID).

**Step 2: Download and Read**
- Use `arxiv_download` to download each selected PDF to `pdfs/<arxiv_id>.pdf`.
- Read the PDF sections relevant to the knowledge gap (Method, Architecture, Experiments).

**Step 3: Create Paper Profile**
Write to `phase0/paper_profiles/<arxiv_id>.md` in **AttributeTree YAML format** (must match the format used by PaperProfiler):
```yaml
---
paper_id: "<arxiv_id>"
title: "<exact title from PDF>"
authors: "[Author1, Author2, ...]"
year: YYYY
venue: "<venue or 'not found in PDF'>"
metadata_source: "pdf_extracted"

method:
  category: "<method_category>"
  description: "<2-3 sentence method summary>"
  architecture:
    agent_count: <number or null>
    coordination_pattern: "<pattern>"
    retrieval_method: "<method>"
    citation_graph_awareness: "<level>"
    citation_graph_detail: "<detail or null>"
    iteration_strategy: "<strategy>"
    llm_backbone: "<model or 'not specified'>"

innovations:
  - name: "<innovation>"
    detail: "<explanation>"

benchmarks:
  - metric: "<metric>"
    value: "<value or null>"
    dataset: "<dataset or null>"
    notes: "<notes or null>"

limitations:
  - "<limitation>"

method_category: "<category>"
category_rationale: "<why this category>"

dimensions:
  graph_awareness_level: "<level>"
  retrieval_type: "<type>"
  writing_paradigm: "<paradigm>"
  evaluation_method: "<method>"
  human_involvement: "<none | optional | mandatory>"
---
```

**Step 4: Update Paper Pool**
- Append to `phase0/02_candidate_pool.md`:
  ```
  - arXiv:XXXX.XXXXX 鈥?Title 鈥?Authors 鈥?Year 鈥?Abstract snippet 鈥?Source: supervisor_retrieval_R{N}
  ```
- Update `phase0/paper_taxonomy.md`: add the new paper to its matching category's paper list.

**Step 5: Update focus_sections.md**
Before writing `focus_sections.md`, enrich each section entry with the retrieval result:
```yaml
  - section: "Section 3: ..."
    issue: "..."
    knowledge_gap: "..."
    search_keywords: [...]
    retrieved_papers:
      - arxiv_id: "XXXX.XXXXX"
        title: "..."
        relevance: "<how it fills the gap>"
```

### Limits
- Maximum 3 new papers total per round
- Only search for sections with `search_keywords` 鈥?not all focus sections need new evidence
- Prefer 2024-2025 papers unless the gap specifically requires older foundational work
- If search surfaces no relevant results, skip and note "no relevant papers found" in the review report
