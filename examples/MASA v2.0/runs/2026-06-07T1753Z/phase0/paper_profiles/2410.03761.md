## Method Description

HiGTL (Hierarchical Graph Taxonomy Learning) is an end-to-end framework that generates hierarchical taxonomy trees directly from citation graphs. Unlike classical taxonomy construction methods that rely on textual content analysis alone, HiGTL leverages the structural information of citation networks — citation relationships, co-citation patterns, and bibliographic coupling — to induce a tree-structured organization of scientific papers. The framework proceeds in two stages: first, it learns paper representations via a graph neural network encoder that aggregates neighborhood information from the citation graph; second, it applies a hierarchical clustering or tree-generation module that organizes papers into nested topic clusters, where each internal node represents a topic or subtopic and leaf nodes represent individual papers or fine-grained research directions.

The key architectural choice is using the citation graph as the primary signal for taxonomy induction, with optional LLM-based node verbalization to label each taxonomy node with human-readable topic descriptors. This enables the system to automatically produce a structured outline of a research field that can serve as a planning skeleton for survey generation. The output taxonomy is a rooted tree where the root represents the entire field, intermediate levels capture increasingly specific sub-areas, and leaves point to relevant papers.

## Key Innovations

- End-to-end taxonomy tree generation directly from citation graph structure without requiring pre-defined topic hierarchies
- Combines graph neural network embeddings with hierarchical clustering for multi-level literature organization
- LLM-based node verbalization to produce human-readable labels for each taxonomy level
- Addresses the gap between flat citation graph analysis and tree-structured survey planning

## Category Rationale

HiGTL enhances standard citation graph analysis with a hierarchical taxonomy extraction layer. Rather than operating as a multi-agent survey generation system, it produces a structured tree of the literature that can be consumed by downstream survey planners. The core innovation is in the graph-to-tree transformation, making it a graph_enhanced_retrieval method that enriches citation graph data with hierarchical organization.

## Limitations

- The taxonomy quality depends heavily on citation graph density and completeness; sparse citation networks may yield poor hierarchies
- Does not incorporate temporal dynamics — the taxonomy is static and reflects the citation graph at a single snapshot
- LLM verbalization step may introduce labeling biases or inconsistencies
- Not evaluated against standard taxonomy/ontology learning benchmarks in available metadata
