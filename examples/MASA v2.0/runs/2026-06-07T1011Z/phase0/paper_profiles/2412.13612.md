## Description

This paper provides a multi-dimensional evaluation of LLM capabilities for automated literature review, measuring hallucination rates, semantic coverage, factual consistency, citation accuracy, and structural coherence across multiple LLMs (GPT-4, Claude, Gemini, Llama) and survey generation approaches.

The evaluation framework uses: (1) Hallucination rate — proportion of generated claims not supported by cited sources; (2) Semantic coverage — how comprehensively the survey covers the topic's key sub-areas; (3) Factual consistency — whether claims are consistent within the survey and with cited sources; (4) Citation precision/recall — accuracy of reference attribution; (5) Structural coherence — organization and flow quality.

Key findings include: GPT-4 achieves the best overall quality but still exhibits significant hallucination rates; citation accuracy is the weakest dimension across all models; semantic coverage varies dramatically by topic familiarity; and no model achieves acceptable performance on all dimensions simultaneously.
