## Method Description

This paper applies GNN architectures to predict citation trends at publication time — forecasting how a new paper's citation count will evolve over its early years. The method processes the citation graph to extract features for each paper: its position in the citation network, the prestige of citing and cited papers, and temporal citation patterns of similar papers published in the same period.

A GNN encoder produces paper representations that capture structural and temporal features, which are then used to forecast citation trajectories. The model is designed to make predictions using only information available at publication time, enabling prospective evaluation of a paper's likely impact. This is relevant for survey agents that need to judge which papers are likely to become influential when deciding inclusion in a survey.

## Category Rationale

This paper uses GNNs for citation trend prediction based on citation graph structure. The graph is the primary input, making it graph_enhanced_retrieval. While focused on prediction rather than retrieval, the methodology is relevant for graph-based literature analysis.

## Limitations

- Predicts citation trends, not paper relevance for surveys
- Accuracy degrades for unusual papers that deviate from typical citation patterns
- Does not incorporate content signals (abstracts, methodology)
- Limited temporal horizon for predictions
