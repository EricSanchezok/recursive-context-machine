## Description

DOVA introduces a deliberation-first multi-agent architecture where agents engage in structured deliberation before any retrieval or generation occurs. This contrasts with the common pattern of retrieve-then-deliberate, where retrieval happens first and deliberation refines the results.

The architecture: (1) Deliberation Phase — multiple agents, each with a different research perspective (empiricist, theoretician, methodologist, domain specialist), discuss the research topic to identify key questions, potential approaches, and information needs before any papers are retrieved; (2) Retrieval Phase — based on the deliberation output, targeted queries are formulated and executed; (3) Synthesis Phase — agents collaborate to produce the literature review based on retrieved papers, guided by the deliberation framework.

The deliberation-first approach ensures that retrieval is guided by well-defined information needs rather than ad-hoc queries, potentially improving retrieval relevance and reducing the need for iterative refinement. The multi-perspective deliberation also surfaces diverse viewpoints that might be missed by a single-agent approach.

## Key Innovations

- Deliberation-first paradigm prioritizing structured discussion before retrieval
- Multi-perspective agent roles (empiricist, theoretician, methodologist) for comprehensive topic analysis
- Deliberation-guided query formulation for targeted retrieval

## Category Rationale

DOVA uses multiple agents with distinct research perspectives that deliberate before retrieval. The deliberation-first paradigm is a novel coordination pattern in multi-agent architectures, distinguishing it from retrieval-first or generation-first approaches.

## Limitations

- No citation graph awareness — retrieval is deliberation-driven
- Single-pass with no iterative refinement after retrieval
- Agent perspective assignment may not capture all relevant viewpoints
- Deliberation phase adds latency before any content generation begins
