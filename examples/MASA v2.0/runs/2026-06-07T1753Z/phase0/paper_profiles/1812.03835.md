## Method Description

This paper presents a graph embedding approach for citation recommendation that constructs task-specific neighborhoods in the citation graph. The key insight is that different citation recommendation tasks (e.g., "find papers that are closely related" vs. "find papers that survey a field") benefit from different definitions of neighborhood in the citation graph. The method proposes constructing custom neighborhoods using a combination of direct citations, co-citation relationships, and bibliographic coupling, with task-specific weights.

The graph embedding is learned via a variant of node2vec or similar random-walk-based embedding method applied to the task-specific graph. Papers that frequently appear together in co-citation or bibliographic coupling contexts receive similar embeddings. The approach is particularly robust when seed papers are scarce — by leveraging multiple types of citation relationships, it can still learn meaningful embeddings even with limited input.

## Limitations

- Pre-dates modern LLMs; does not leverage semantic understanding
- Task-specific neighborhood construction requires manual configuration per task
- Random-walk embeddings may not capture long-range dependencies in the citation graph
- Evaluated on pre-2023 benchmarks that may not reflect current state-of-the-art
