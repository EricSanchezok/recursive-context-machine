## Method Description

LitLLMs proposes a two-step retrieval approach for literature review using LLMs: (1) keyword extraction — an LLM extracts key terms and concepts from the user's research question, and (2) retrieval and re-ranking — retrieved papers are re-ranked using an LLM-based relevance assessment. The retrieval stage uses the extracted keywords for initial search (over bibliographic databases), and the re-ranking stage applies LLM scoring to prioritize the most relevant papers.

The paper also presents a planning-based generation approach where the LLM generates a survey plan (outline) based on the retrieved literature, followed by section-by-section generation with inline citations. Importantly, the paper includes a comprehensive evaluation protocol that assesses survey quality across multiple dimensions: coverage, citation accuracy, readability, and factual correctness. The findings serve as a benchmark for current LLM capabilities in literature review tasks, identifying gaps and limitations.

## Category Rationale

LitLLMs uses a single LLM pipeline for keyword extraction, retrieval re-ranking, planning, and generation. Despite multiple stages, a single LLM orchestrates all steps without specialized multi-agent coordination, placing it in single_agent_pipeline.

## Limitations

- No citation graph traversal for literature discovery
- Single-pass generation without iterative refinement
- Evaluation reveals significant coverage and citation accuracy gaps
- Keyword extraction quality limits downstream retrieval performance
