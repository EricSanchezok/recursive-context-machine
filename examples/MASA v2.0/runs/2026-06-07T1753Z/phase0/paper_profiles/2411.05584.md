## Method Description

This paper investigates the Matthew Effect in citations — the phenomenon where well-known researchers and prestigious venues receive disproportionately more citations than quality alone would predict. The study predicts citations from pre-publication variables only (author prestige, venue prestige, institution, funding) to quantify the prestige effect separately from paper quality.

The findings show that prestige variables explain a significant portion of citation variance even before publication, indicating that citation counts reflect prestige as much as quality. This has implications for survey systems that use citation counts as relevance or quality signals — they may inherit and amplify prestige bias, systematically over-citing well-known authors and venues.
