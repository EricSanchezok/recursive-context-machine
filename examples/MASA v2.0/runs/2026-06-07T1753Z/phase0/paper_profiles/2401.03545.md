## Method Description

This paper provides a counterpoint to the citation amnesia narrative, arguing that the observed recency trend in NLP citations reflects genuine field dynamics rather than bias per se. The authors analyze citation patterns and argue that: (1) NLP is a young, rapidly evolving field where older work may genuinely be less relevant to current research, (2) the field's growth means there are more recent papers to cite, not that older papers are being unfairly neglected, and (3) controlling for field maturity and growth rate reduces the apparent recency bias.

The paper provides a nuanced perspective on citation age trends, suggesting that recency in citations may partly reflect legitimate scholarly choices rather than systematic bias. This has implications for automated survey systems — an exclusive focus on recent papers may be appropriate for fast-moving fields.
