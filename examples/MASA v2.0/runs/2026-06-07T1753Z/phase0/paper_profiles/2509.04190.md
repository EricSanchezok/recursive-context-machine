## Method Description

This paper conducts a large-scale full-text analysis of 900 highly-cited papers (HCPs) and 220,000 citing papers to study how the role of cited papers changes over time. The analysis reveals a shift from topical referencing (citing a paper for its content relevance) to symbolic referencing (citing a paper as a marker of a research area or methodology, regardless of specific content).

The study finds that as papers age, they are increasingly cited symbolically — as landmarks or exemplars of a field — rather than for specific content contributions. This has important implications for automated survey systems: symbolic citations may incorrectly indicate topical relevance when in fact the cited paper is serving as a field marker rather than a topical source. Survey systems need to distinguish between topical and symbolic citations.

## Limitations

- Focused on highly-cited papers — may not generalize to all papers
- Citation role categorization may have subjective elements
- Does not provide methods for distinguishing topical vs. symbolic citations in practice
- Full-text analysis limited to papers where full text is available
