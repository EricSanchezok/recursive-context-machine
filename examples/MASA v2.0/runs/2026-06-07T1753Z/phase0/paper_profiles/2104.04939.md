## Method Description

This paper proposes a Graph Convolutional Network (GCN) framework for estimating the future citation count of academic papers. The method models the citation graph as a directed graph where nodes are papers and edges represent citation relationships. A multi-layer GCN encoder aggregates information from each paper's citation neighborhood — papers that cite it and papers it cites — to produce a paper representation that captures its structural role in the citation network.

The GCN output is fed into a regression head that predicts future citation counts at one or more future time horizons. The framework is trained on historical citation data: given the citation graph at time T, it predicts citation counts at time T+Δt. Key features include handling of directed citation edges (distinguishing between citing and being cited) and the ability to incorporate paper metadata (venue, author count, etc.) as node features alongside structural information.

## Category Rationale

This is a citation graph analysis method using GCNs for prediction. The graph structure is central to the approach, making it graph_enhanced_retrieval. While focused on prediction rather than retrieval, the methodology for graph-based paper representation is directly relevant to retrieval tasks.

## Limitations

- Predicts citation counts, not paper relevance or survey content — a different task
- Static graph assumption; does not model how the graph itself evolves
- Prediction quality degrades for papers with very short or very long citation histories
- Not directly applicable to survey generation without significant adaptation
