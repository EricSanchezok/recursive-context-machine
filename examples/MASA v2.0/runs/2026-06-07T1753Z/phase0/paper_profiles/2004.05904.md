## Method Description

This paper demonstrates that direct citation replication clustering outperforms NLP-based clustering for organizing scientific literature. Direct citation replication clusters papers by measuring how often they are cited together (co-citation strength), finding that this simple structural measure produces more coherent and practically useful clusters than sophisticated NLP-based approaches using text similarity.

The finding is significant for survey systems: it suggests that citation graph structure can be a more reliable signal for literature organization than text content analysis alone. The paper recommends that survey systems should prioritize citation-based clustering over text-based clustering for organizing literature into topics.
