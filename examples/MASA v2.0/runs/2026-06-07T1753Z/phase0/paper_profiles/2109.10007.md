## Method Description

This paper extends bibliographic coupling to deep neighborhoods using graph diffusion. Standard bibliographic coupling measures similarity between two papers based on how many references they share. Deep bibliographic coupling extends this concept by using graph diffusion — propagating coupling scores through multiple hops in the citation graph — to capture indirect relationships between papers that may not share references directly but are connected through intermediate papers.

The diffusion process generates a "local map of science" around a seed paper, showing how papers cluster into research fronts and how those fronts relate to each other. The deep coupling approach captures structural similarity that would be missed by direct bibliographic coupling, providing a richer picture of the research landscape.

## Limitations

- Graph diffusion may be computationally expensive for large graphs
- Multi-hop propagation may introduce noise from irrelevant papers
- Evaluated on clustering, not survey generation
- Does not incorporate text content for semantic understanding
