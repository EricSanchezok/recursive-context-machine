## Method Description

This paper provides a definitive large-scale study of recency bias in citation practices, analyzing 240 million papers across 20 academic fields over 43 years. The key finding is "citation age recession" — the average age of cited references has been steadily decreasing, meaning researchers increasingly cite only recent work while older foundational work is cited less and less.

The study quantifies this trend across fields, finding that NLP and computer science exhibit the strongest recency bias while humanities and social sciences show more stable citation age distributions. The paper discusses causes (rapid publication growth, pressure to cite recent work, search engine biases) and implications (reinventing the wheel, loss of cumulative knowledge, distorted scientific progress narratives). This has direct implications for automated survey systems — they may inherit and amplify this recency bias if not explicitly counteracted.

## Limitations

- Observational study — does not propose solutions for recency bias
- Analysis at field level may mask within-field variation
- Does not consider citation context (are older papers cited for foundational or decorative reasons?)
- Data source coverage may skew toward English-language publications
