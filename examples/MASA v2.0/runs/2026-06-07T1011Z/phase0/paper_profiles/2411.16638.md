## Description

This paper provides a systematic critique and stress-test of automatic factuality metrics used for evaluating LLM-generated scientific text. It reveals that commonly used metrics (including FActScore, entailment-based metrics, and LLM-as-a-Judge) can be systematically gamed by exploiting shallow features of the metrics rather than improving actual factual accuracy.

The study identifies specific failure modes: (1) Length bias — longer text with more hedging language scores higher on factuality metrics even when factual density is lower; (2) Abstention gaming — models that say "I don't know" more often score higher even though they provide less useful information; (3) Circular verification — verification against the same knowledge source used for training creates artificially high scores; (4) Surface form sensitivity — minor paraphrasing can change scores dramatically without changing factual content.

The critique is significant for survey generation evaluation because it cautions against over-reliance on any single metric and provides guidelines for more robust evaluation design.
