## Method Description

SurveyGen introduces a quality-aware RAG framework (QUAL-SG) for survey generation that explicitly models survey quality dimensions during the generation process. The system is built around two key components: a quality estimation module that predicts the quality of a generated survey across multiple dimensions (coverage, coherence, citation accuracy, readability), and a quality-guided generation module that uses these predictions to guide the writing process.

The architecture proceeds through: (1) Retrieval — papers are gathered via hybrid search and citation graph traversal from seed papers; (2) Quality-Aware Outline Generation — the system generates candidate outlines and scores each for predicted quality, selecting the highest-scoring one; (3) Quality-Guided Section Drafting — each section is drafted with explicit attention to quality dimensions, guided by the quality model; (4) Quality Estimation and Refinement — the completed draft is scored and specific sections below quality thresholds are revised.

SurveyGen also contributes a large-scale dataset of 4,200 human-written surveys across diverse domains with 242K references, which serves as training data for the quality estimation model. The quality-aware approach is notable for making quality evaluation an integral part of the generation process rather than a post-hoc assessment.

- Large-scale training dataset of 4,200 human-written surveys with 242K references
- Targeted refinement triggered by per-dimension quality scores rather than uniform revision

## Category Rationale

SurveyGen combines citation graph traversal in its retrieval phase with quality-aware generation. The citation graph component ensures broader paper discovery beyond keyword search, while the quality estimation model provides iterative refinement guidance. The graph awareness, though not hierarchical, is an integral part of the retrieval strategy that distinguishes it from purely search-based approaches.

## Limitations

- Quality estimation model requires training data (4,200 surveys) — may not generalize to domains outside the training distribution
- Citation graph traversal is single-level (BFS) — no multi-hop expansion
- Refinement loop is limited to sections below quality threshold, not a full iterative rewrite
- Quality dimensions are pre-defined — may not capture all aspects of survey quality across domains
