## Method Description

This paper proposes a hierarchical tree-structured knowledge graph designed specifically for helping beginner researchers conduct academic insight surveys. The knowledge graph organizes scientific literature into a tree structure where each node represents a research topic or sub-topic, and edges represent inheritance relationships (parent→child: topic→subtopic) and relevance relationships (cross-links between related topics at the same level). The hierarchy is designed to reflect how a human expert would mentally organize a research field — moving from broad areas to specific sub-problems.

The construction pipeline extracts entity-relationship triples from scientific papers (titles, abstracts, full text where available), organizes them into a hierarchy using a combination of rule-based pattern matching and embedding similarity clustering, and then presents the resulting tree to beginner researchers as a navigable survey interface. The tree provides both a high-level overview (broad topic areas) and drill-down capability (specific methods, datasets, and findings) to support literature exploration.

## Category Rationale

This paper constructs a hierarchical knowledge graph from scientific literature to support survey exploration. It enhances standard content-based retrieval with graph structure and hierarchical organization, making it a graph_enhanced_retrieval method. It does not involve multi-agent coordination or iterative generation.

## Limitations

- Designed for beginner researchers — may oversimplify for expert-level survey needs
- Relies on rule-based pattern matching which may not generalize across disciplines
- Static tree structure does not adapt to user interests or evolving research landscapes
- No integration with LLM-based synthesis or generation capabilities
