## Method Description

Graphs of Research proposes using 2-hop citation graph DAGs (Directed Acyclic Graphs) as supervision signals for training models to generate research ideas. The core insight is that the citation evolution structure — which papers build upon which, and how ideas flow through the citation graph — encodes a natural curriculum for research ideation. The method extracts subgraphs from the full citation graph centered on a focal paper, retaining its immediate predecessors (papers it cites) and successors (papers that cite it) within a 2-hop window.

These citation evolution DAGs are used to construct training examples for supervised fine-tuning (SFT) of LLMs: given a set of precursor papers (the "prior art"), the model is trained to predict or generate the next research direction — effectively learning the logical progression of scientific ideas from the citation structure. The approach reframes survey and ideation as a graph-constrained generation task, where the citation graph provides the structural supervision that guides what constitutes a plausible research trajectory.

## Category Rationale

Graphs of Research uses citation graph structure as supervision (SFT training signal) for idea generation. The citation graph is central to the method's training paradigm, making it a graph_enhanced_retrieval approach. It does not operate as a multi-agent pipeline or an interactive system.

## Limitations

- Limited to 2-hop citation neighborhoods; may miss broader research context
- Quality of generated ideas depends on citation graph completeness and accuracy
- The SFT-based approach may reinforce existing citation biases and echo chambers
- No evaluation on standard survey or idea generation benchmarks reported in available metadata
