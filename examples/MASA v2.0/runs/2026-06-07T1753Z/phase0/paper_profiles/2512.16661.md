## Method Description

This paper presents an attention-based subgraph retriever that combines GNN and LLM components for literature retrieval from the Microsoft Academic Graph (MAG). The system operates by: (1) taking a seed paper or topic query, (2) extracting a relevant subgraph from the full MAG citation graph (typically 2-3 hops), (3) encoding the subgraph using a GNN with attention mechanisms that weigh different citation relationships, and (4) using an LLM to interpret the subgraph features and generate ranked retrieval results.

The GNN component captures structural signals — which papers are central in the subgraph, which citation paths are most important, and how papers cluster topically. The LLM component adds semantic understanding, interpreting paper titles and abstracts within the context of the subgraph structure. The attention mechanism within the GNN learns which types of citation edges (direct citations, co-citations, bibliographic coupling) are most informative for the specific retrieval query, allowing the system to adapt its subgraph interpretation to different research domains and query types.

## Category Rationale

## Limitations

- Depends on Microsoft Academic Graph availability (MAG is no longer actively maintained)
- Subgraph extraction (2-3 hops) may miss relevant papers in less-connected areas
- LLM interpretation step adds computational cost and latency
- Does not generate surveys or structured literature reviews — retrieval only
