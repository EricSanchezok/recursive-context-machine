## Method Description

DACR (Dual Attention Model for Citation Recommendation) combines self-attention and additive attention to capture word-level, section-level, and structural contexts for citation recommendation. The model takes a query paper and candidate papers, and computes relevance scores by attending over multiple levels of granularity: (1) word-level self-attention captures important terms within sentence contexts, (2) section-level attention weighs the contribution of different sections (introduction vs. methodology vs. related work), and (3) structural context from the citation graph provides additional relational signals.

The dual attention mechanism combines self-attention (which captures relationships between all pairs of words in the input) with additive attention (which computes importance weights for different parts of the input). This allows the model to capture both fine-grained terminological relevance and coarse-grained structural and section-level relevance.

## Category Rationale

DACR uses a combination of self-attention and additive attention for citation recommendation, incorporating structural context from the citation graph. The graph provides supplementary features alongside text content, making it a graph_enhanced_retrieval method.

## Limitations

- Pre-dates modern LLMs; attention capacity limited compared to current models
- Structural context from citation graph is supplementary, not primary
- Does not address multi-hop traversal or survey-level synthesis
- Section-level attention requires well-structured full text as input
