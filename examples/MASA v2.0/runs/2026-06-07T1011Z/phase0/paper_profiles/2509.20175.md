## Description

This paper proposes a federated multi-agent architecture where agents operate semi-autonomously on different subsets of the literature and communicate through a semantic coordination layer. Unlike centralized orchestration, each agent in the federation has autonomy over its assigned paper subset and contributes structured insights to a shared semantic space.

The architecture: (1) Paper Partitioning — the literature corpus is partitioned into semantic clusters (by topic, method, or approach); (2) Agent Assignment — each agent is assigned a cluster and analyzes it independently; (3) Semantic Contribution — agents contribute structured findings (key claims, methods, relationships) to a shared knowledge graph; (4) Synthesis — a coordinator agent traverses the shared knowledge graph to produce the integrated survey.

The semantics-aware coordination means agents communicate through structured knowledge graph updates rather than natural language messages, enabling more precise and composable information exchange. The federated approach allows agents to specialize on specific literature subsets while the shared semantic space enables global consistency.

## Category Rationale

The federated architecture with autonomous agents, shared semantic space, and knowledge graph-based coordination is a novel multi-agent pattern. Agents specialize on literature clusters and communicate through structured knowledge graph updates.

## Limitations

- Paper partitioning quality determines overall coverage
- Knowledge graph construction requires careful schema design
- Coordinator must effectively traverse the shared graph for synthesis
- Autonomous agents may miss cross-cluster connections visible only through coordination
