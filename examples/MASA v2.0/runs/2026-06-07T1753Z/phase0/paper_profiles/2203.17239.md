## Method Description

This paper studies citation bias in academic peer review, finding a +0.23 score increase when reviewers encounter citations to their own work in the manuscript under review. The study analyzes reviewer scores and citation patterns to quantify how citations to a reviewer's own papers influence their evaluation.

The finding has implications for survey generation: if surveys cite certain papers, the authors of those papers may evaluate the survey more favorably. More importantly, it highlights that citation practices are not purely meritocratic — they are influenced by social and strategic factors. Automated survey systems should be aware of this when evaluating citation-based quality signals.
