## Method Description

This paper critically examines existing literature search evaluation practices, finding that only 51% of human-constructed citation lists are judged relevant, revealing significant co-authorship bias in human citation practices. The study analyzes citation lists from research papers and finds that authors tend to cite their own work and their collaborators' work at rates beyond what relevance alone would explain.

The finding has profound implications for survey generation evaluation: if human-written surveys have citation lists where ~49% of citations are not strictly relevant (by expert judgment), then evaluating automated surveys against human citation patterns is problematic. The paper proposes new evaluation approaches that account for citation bias and measure genuine relevance rather than matching human citation patterns.
