## Method Description

This paper analyzes citation behavior by examining social and semantic ties, finding that collaboration network proximity is the strongest predictor of citation. The study examines: (1) social ties — co-authorship relationships, advisor-advisee relationships, institutional affiliations, (2) semantic ties — topic similarity, methodological overlap, and (3) citation behavior — whether these ties predict citation likelihood.

The finding that collaboration-network proximity is the strongest predictor has implications for survey systems: citation graphs may reflect social relationships more than topical relevance. Survey systems that rely on citation graph traversal may systematically over-cite the social circles of well-connected authors and under-cite independent researchers.
