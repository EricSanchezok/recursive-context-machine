## Method Description

This paper presents a GNN-based architecture for mapping interdisciplinary research networks by learning graph embeddings that preserve cross-domain citation relationships. The method models the citation graph as a heterogeneous network where papers from different disciplines are distinct node types, and cross-disciplinary citations are special edge types. A heterogeneous graph neural network (HGNN) encoder learns embeddings that capture both intra-disciplinary structure (citations within a field) and inter-disciplinary bridges (citations across fields).

The training objective encourages embeddings to preserve cross-domain proximity — papers from different disciplines that are frequently co-cited or that cite each other should have similar embeddings. This allows the model to identify interdisciplinary research fronts and emerging cross-domain connections. The approach is particularly relevant for survey generation in cross-disciplinary topics, where standard within-field citation analysis would miss important cross-domain linkages.

## Key Innovations

- Heterogeneous graph neural network explicitly designed for cross-disciplinary citation relationships
- Embedding objective that preserves interdisciplinary bridges, not just within-discipline structure
- Enables identification of research fronts that span multiple fields

## Category Rationale

This paper uses a heterogeneous GNN to learn cross-disciplinary citation embeddings. The graph is central to the method — citation relationships, including cross-domain edges, are the primary input signal. This places it clearly in the graph_enhanced_retrieval category as a method that enhances retrieval through graph structure.

## Limitations

- Requires discipline labels for papers, which may not always be available or clean
- Cross-disciplinary edges may be sparse, making training difficult for less-connected fields
- Does not incorporate temporal dynamics or recency weighting
- No direct integration with survey generation or synthesis
