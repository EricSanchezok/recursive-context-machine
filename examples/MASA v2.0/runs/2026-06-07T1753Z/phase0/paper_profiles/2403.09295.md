## Method Description

This paper provides a systematic comparison of three seed-based information retrieval methods using citation relationships: (1) direct citations (papers that cite or are cited by the seed), (2) bibliographic coupling (papers that share references with the seed), and (3) co-citations (papers that are cited together with the seed). The study evaluates each method individually and in combination across multiple datasets.

The key finding: combining all three methods outperforms any single method for seed-based literature retrieval. Direct citations provide the most precise but narrowest results, bibliographic coupling provides broader but noisier results, and co-citations capture related work that neither direct citations nor coupling would find. The paper provides practical guidance for configuring citation-based retrieval in survey systems.
