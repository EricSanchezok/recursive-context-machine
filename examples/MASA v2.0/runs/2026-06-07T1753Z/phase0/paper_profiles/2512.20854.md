# Paper Profile: How important is Recall for Measuring Retrieval Quality?

- **arXiv ID**: 2512.20854
- **Authors**: Shelly Schwartz, Oleg Vasilyev, Randy Sawaya
- **Year**: 2025

## Method Description
This paper investigates a fundamental question for the retrieval-augmented generation pipeline: when the total number of relevant documents is unknown (as it almost always is in realistic settings), recall cannot be computed, yet retrieval quality must still be measured. The authors evaluate several established strategies for handling this limitation by measuring the **correlation between retrieval quality metrics and LLM-based judgments of response quality**, where responses are generated from the retrieved documents. They conduct experiments across multiple datasets with a relatively low number of relevant documents (2–15). They also introduce a simple retrieval quality measure that performs well without requiring knowledge of the total number of relevant documents.

## Architecture
- Retrieval quality metrics: precision@k, NDCG, MRR, and the proposed measure
- Response quality: LLM-based judgments of generated text quality
- Correlation analysis between retrieval metrics and generation quality
- Datasets with known relevant document counts (2–15 per query)

## Key Innovations
- Directly measures the correlation (not assumption) between retrieval quality and generation quality
- Provides empirical evidence for which retrieval metrics best predict downstream generation quality
- Proposes a recall-free retrieval quality metric that correlates with generation quality
- Direct evidence relevant to the bottleneck transfer problem (§3.4)

## Benchmark Results
| Dataset | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| Multiple IR datasets | Correlation between retrieval metrics and LLM-judged response quality varies significantly by metric |
| Multiple IR datasets | Proposed recall-free measure performs competitively with metrics that require full recall knowledge |

## Method Category
`benchmark_evaluation` / `analysis`. This is an analytical paper studying the relationship between retrieval quality and generation quality, directly relevant to the bottleneck transfer problem.

## Relevance to Knowledge Gap
Directly addresses the bottleneck transfer knowledge gap: provides the first empirical evidence on whether retrieval quality metrics correlate with generation quality. Demonstrates that the correlation is metric-dependent and not guaranteed. Supports the argument in §3.4 that the field cannot assume retrieval improvements translate to survey quality improvements.
