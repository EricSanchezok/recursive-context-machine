## Method Description

SurveyG introduces a three-tier hierarchical citation graph structure — Foundation, Development, and Frontier — to organize papers during survey generation. This is a core architectural contribution: rather than treating all retrieved papers uniformly, SurveyG explicitly models the citation graph to discover and categorize papers by their role in the research lineage.

The workflow begins with a seed set of papers identified via topic search. The system then performs citation graph traversal: backward chaining (following references from seed papers) to identify foundational works (Foundation tier) and development works (Development tier), and forward chaining (finding papers that cite seed papers) to discover frontier works (Frontier tier). This creates a three-layer hierarchical taxonomy of the literature.

A multi-agent framework coordinates the generation: a Planning Agent designs the survey structure based on the hierarchical graph; a Retrieval Agent manages the graph traversal and paper collection; a Writing Agent drafts content organized by the three-tier structure; and a Validation Agent checks citation accuracy and content quality. The hierarchical graph enables the survey to naturally present literature progression from foundations through developments to cutting-edge frontiers, mirroring how human-written surveys typically organize material.

## Key Innovations

- First system to explicitly model citation graph hierarchy (three tiers) for survey organization
- Bidirectional citation graph traversal (forward + backward chaining) from seed papers
- Tier-aware survey writing that mirrors human literature review progression
- Multi-agent coordination centered on citation graph structure

## Category Rationale

SurveyG's primary innovation is the explicit modeling of hierarchical citation graph structure to organize survey content. While it uses multiple agents, the core architectural contribution is the graph-based retrieval and organization strategy. The three-tier (Foundation/Development/Frontier) graph is the central organizing principle that drives the entire pipeline.

## Limitations

- Single-pass graph construction — no iterative refinement of the graph during writing
- Graph traversal limited to one level of forward/backward chaining (no multi-hop expansion)
- Validation agent checks citations but does not verify factual accuracy of claims attributed to cited papers
- Evaluation limited to computer science topics
