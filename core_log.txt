KVP [2602.10238] and ForesightKV [2602.03203] require RL-based training, which may limit their applicability to model families where training infrastructure is available.\\n\\n### 4.6 Open Problems\\n\\nThe generalizability of per-head allocation across architectures remains unresolved. The retrieval head phenomenon was discovered in LLaMA-family models, and it is not yet clear whether Mistral, Falcon, or GPT architectures exhibit the same functional specialization. Furthermore, the interaction between attention-guided allocation and advanced attention mechanisms (e.g., MLA in DeepSeek-V3, sliding window + global attention in Mistral) is underexplored.\\n\\nThe connection to interpretability is an emerging theme: attention head analysis for compression simultaneously reveals functional specialization, suggesting that compression and interpretability research may be mutually reinforcing.\\n\\n**Key references**: [2404.14469, 2407.15891, 2407.11550, 2410.00161, 2410.19258, 2502.13176, 2502.16886, 2508.02401, 2509.05165, 2509.09754, 2509.03136, 2510.00636, 2507.08143, 2603.04411, 2509.08315, 2602.08686, 2412.09036, 2309.06180]\\n\\n---\\n\\n## Section 5: Merging, Hybrid, and Cross-Layer Compression\\n\\n**Subtitle**: Composite representations 鈥?merging KV tokens, cross-layer sharing, and hybrid quantization-eviction recipes\\n\\n### 5.1 The Merging Paradigm\\n\\nMerging-based compression combines multiple KV entries into composite representations, reducing the number of entries while preserving information from all of them. This differs from eviction (which discards entries) and quantization (which preserves all entries at degraded precision). Merging operates on the assumption that KV entries in certain neighborhoods 鈥?whether along the sequence axis, the depth axis, or across layers 鈥?contain redundant information that can be consolidated.\\n\\n**KVMerger** [2407.08454] pioneered adaptive merging by selecting which KV entries to merge using Gaussian kernel-weighted averaging. The model itself guides the merging decisions through an analysis of attention patterns, identifying entries that are likely to be attended to together.\\n\\n### 5.2 Depth-Dimension Merging\\n\\n**MiniCache** [2405.14366] made the surprising observation that KV entries across adjacent Transformer layers exhibit high similarity, particularly in deeper layers. By merging KV entries along the depth dimension (across layers), MiniCache reduces the cache without inference overhead or training. It disentangles magnitude and direction components of KV vectors, showing that the direction component carries most of the task-relevant information while magnitude is more compressible.\\n\\n**KVSlimmer** [2603.00907] provides a theoretical analysis of why depth-dimension merging works, showing that the asymmetry in KL divergence between layers determines merging feasibility. It uses spectral energy analysis to identify which layers can be safely merged without gradient computation. **xKV** [2503.18893] extends depth merging to cross-layer SVD, aligning singular vectors across layers to enable a compact joint basis for KV representation, achieving 6.8脳 compression.\\n\\n### 5.3 Hybrid Quantization-Eviction Recipes\\n\\nHybrid methods recognize that quantization and eviction have complementary failure modes and can be combined synergistically. **GEAR** [2403.05527] is the prototypical hybrid recipe, combining three components: (1) quantization of the majority of KV entries, (2) a low-rank approximation to capture the residual error, and (3) a sparse encoding of outlier entries that cannot be captured by either quantization or low-rank approximation. This three-way decomposition allows GEAR to achieve near-lossless compression at 4鈥?脳 ratios.\\n\\n**RDKV** [2605.08317] frames joint eviction and quantization as a rate-distortion optimization problem, allocating bits between \\\"which tokens to keep\\\" (eviction) and \\\"how precisely to represent them\\\" (quantization). This unified formulation yields a 9.1% improvement over the best single-axis baseline. **EVICPRESS** [2512.14946] defines a unified utility function across eviction and compression decisions, combined with multi-tier storage management that places low-utility entries on slower storage tiers.\\n\\n**MiKV** [2402.18096] retains evicted KV pairs in low-precision format rather than discarding them entirely, ensuring that no token is completely lost. It includes safety and hallucination analysis, showing that aggressive eviction can increase hallucination rates and that retaining low-precision copies of evicted entries mitigates this risk.\\n\\n### 5.4 Cross-Layer Architectural Sharing\\n\\nA distinct approach reduces KV cache at the architecture level rather than through post-hoc compression. **Cross-Layer Attention (CLA)** [2405.12981] shares key and value projections between adjacent layers, effectively halving the number of KV entries stored 鈥?the KV cache of layer *l* serves layers *l* and *l+1*. This 2脳 reduction is orthogonal to MQA/GQA and can be combined with them for compounding gains.\\n\\n**KVSharer** [2410.18517] makes the counterintuitive observation that sharing across _dissimilar_ layers (measured by KL divergence of their attention distributions) can be more effective than sharing across similar layers, because dissimilar layers are less likely to need the same KV entries simultaneously. **LRKV** [2601.11471] introduces a shared full-rank KV representation with low-rank head-specific residuals, subsuming MQA and GQA as special cases and providing a flexible framework for KV cache reduction.\\n\\n### 5.5 Transform Coding and Sparse Coding\\n\\nTransform coding methods apply learned or fixed transformations to KV representations for compact storage. **KVTC** [2511.01815] applies PCA to KV vectors to identify principal components, followed by adaptive quantization of coefficients and entropy coding 鈥?achieving 20脳 compression on the AIME25 and LiveCodeBench tasks. **Lexico** [2412.08890] uses sparse coding over a universal dictionary, representing each KV entry as a sparse combination of dictionary atoms and achieving 15鈥?5% memory at 90鈥?5% performance retention.\\n\\n**KV-CAR** [2512.06727] combines autoencoder-based compression with similarity-driven reuse across adjacent layers, identifying that KV representations from adjacent layers can be decoded from shared latent representations.\\n\\n### 5.6 Synergy or Diminishing Returns?\\n\\nA critical question for hybrid methods is whether combining multiple compression axes yields synergy or saturates diminishing returns. The evidence to date is mixed: GEAR's three-way decomposition clearly outperforms any single component in isolation, suggesting synergy. However, at extreme compression ratios (>10脳), the interactions between quantization noise and eviction error can compound rather than cancel. The optimal joint allocation across eviction, quantization, and merging dimensions remains an open optimization problem.\\n\\n### 5.7 Open Problems\\n\\nTraining-free methods like MiniCache and CLA are attractive because they avoid fine-tuning costs, but their compression ratios are limited (typically 2鈥?脳). Achieving extreme compression (>8脳) currently requires training, hybrid recipes, or both. Finding training-free methods that combine cross-layer sharing with post-hoc compression is an active research direction.\\n\\n**Key references**: [2403.05527, 2405.14366, 2407.08454, 2603.04427, 2504.09936, 2603.00907, 2503.18893, 2503.01330, 2507.11273, 2507.06517, 2512.06727, 2601.11471, 2405.12981, 2601.03067, 2412.02252, 2410.18517, 2509.21623, 2605.08317, 2512.14946, 2402.18096, 2511.01815, 2605.08840, 2412.08890, 2603.16435, 2511.01815]\\n\\n---\\n\\n## Section 6: Mechanism, Theory, and Fundamental Limits\\n\\n**Subtitle**: Understanding why compression works 鈥?information-theoretic bounds, error propagation, and the premise-challenging argument\\n\\n### 6.1 Information-Theoretic Compression Bounds\\n\\nTheoretical work has established fundamental limits on how much the KV cache can be compressed. **Compression Barriers for Autoregressive Transformers** [2502.15955] proves that any KV compression algorithm must use 螛(nd) space in the worst case, where *n* is sequence length and *d* is the key/value dimension. This is a negative result: there is no free lunch. However, the bound is worst-case; typical attention distributions may be far more compressible.\\n\\n**Polynomial Context-Truncation Sensitivity** [2605.25085] provides sequential Wyner-Ziv bounds showing that the sensitivity of the model's output to truncated context decays polynomially (not exponentially) with the truncation window. This means that while context always matters, its marginal importance decreases 鈥?providing a theoretical foundation for why sliding-window and eviction methods can work despite discarding large portions of the context.\\n\\n**How Much Cache Does Reasoning Need?** [2604.17935] analyzes the specific case of multi-step reasoning, proving that certain reasoning patterns (particularly pointer-chaining and multi-hop retrieval) require KV cache size that scales with the number of reasoning steps. This establishes a lower bound for compression of reasoning traces that is higher than the bound for simple retrieval.\\n\\n### 6.2 Error Propagation Analysis\\n\\nCompression errors in early decoding steps can compound as generation proceeds. **VeriCache** [2605.17613] provides a practical solution: it verifies compressed-KV drafts against the full (uncompressed) KV computation and recomputes on mismatch, guaranteeing identical outputs to full-cache generation. This bridges the gap between lossy compression and lossless inference, albeit at the cost of occasional recomputation overhead.\\n\\n**Moment-KV** [2605.29873] analyzes attention dynamics and proposes momentum-driven temporal aggregation that smooths out quantization noise across decoding steps, showing that noise at individual steps is less harmful than its cumulative effect. **StiefAttention** [2601.21686] formulates low-rank KV approximation as optimization over the Stiefel manifold, directly minimizing decoder-layer output reconstruction error rather than KV reconstruction error 鈥?a more faithful objective for downstream task preservation.\\n\\n**Q-Filters** [2503.02812] leverages Query-Key geometry to efficiently approximate attention without storing the full KV cache, achieving 99% NIAH accuracy at 32脳 compression through a training-free FlashAttention-compatible method.\\n\\n### 6.3 Sparsity and Attention Dynamics\\n\\nSeveral works analyze the intrinsic sparsity of attention distributions. **SP-KV** [2605.14037] (Self-Pruned Key-Value Attention) learns when to _not_ write to the cache by predicting future token utility, revealing structured sparsity patterns where only a fraction of tokens are needed for future attention. **FASA** [2602.03152] (Frequency-aware Sparse Attention) discovers that different RoPE frequency bands dominate attention at different generation phases and that query-aware token eviction can achieve near-oracle accuracy.\\n\\n**SPHERICAL KV** [2605.18856] parameterizes KV states on a sphere (angle-domain representation) and shows that angle-domain attention can be computed without dense key reconstruction, reducing both storage and compute simultaneously.\\n\\n### 6.4 The Premise-Challenging Argument\\n\\nPerhaps the most provocative contribution to the theoretical literature is **KV-Direct** [2603.19664], which argues that the KV cache is _information-theoretically redundant_ given the residual stream. The paper proves that the key and value tensors can be recomputed from the residual stream with zero error 鈥?meaning that lossless KV cache compression is theoretically possible by storing only the residual stream and recomputing KV on demand. If this result holds across architectures, it would fundamentally change the compression landscape: instead of \\\"how much can we compress the KV cache?\\\" the question becomes \\\"how efficiently can we recompute KV from residuals?\\\"\\n\\nKV-Direct's argument is supported by **Quantization Dominates Rank Reduction** [2604.11501], which provides an analytical comparison showing that quantization consistently outperforms low-rank decomposition (a common alternative compression axis) when compared on a Fisher-metric-optimal distortion measure. This suggests that the community's focus on quantization over low-rank methods is theoretically justified.\\n\\nAt a more extreme scale, **Cramming 1568 Tokens into a Single Vector** [2502.13063] empirically explores the limits of embedding space capacity and demonstrates that information-theoretic compression ratios of 1500脳 exist in embedding space, though the reconstruction fidelity for downstream tasks at such extremes is yet to be evaluated.\\n\\n### 6.5 Open Theoretical Questions\\n\\nSeveral fundamental questions remain open. First, is there a unified information-theoretic framework that connects quantization, eviction, and merging under a common rate-distortion objective? CapKV [2604.25975] and RDKV [2605.08317] take steps in this direction but have not yet produced a fully general theory. Second, KV-Direct's premise needs independent verification across architecture families (LLaMA, Mistral, GPT) to determine whether KV cache redundancy is universal or architecture-specific. Third, the relationship between compression-induced error and downstream task degradation remains poorly characterized 鈥?current metrics (perplexity, accuracy on LongBench) are coarse-grained and may miss subtle failure modes.\\n\\n**Key references**: [2604.11501, 2605.14037, 2602.03152, 2605.18856, 2605.17613, 2605.29873, 2503.02812, 2509.00579, 2506.04642, 2601.21686, 2604.17935, 2605.25085, 2502.15955, 2605.21226, 2605.11478, 2603.19664, 2603.10899, 2502.13063]\\n\\n---\\n\\n## Section 7: Evaluation 鈥?Benchmarks, Metrics, and Protocols\\n\\n**Subtitle**: How KV cache compression methods are measured 鈥?standard benchmarks, reliability concerns, and emerging evaluation frameworks\\n\\n### 7.1 Standard Benchmarks\\n\\nTwo benchmarks serve as the de facto standard for KV cache compression evaluation.\\n\\n**LongBench** [2308.14508] provides a bilingual (English/Chinese) multi-task benchmark with 21 tasks across 6 categories: single-doc QA, multi-doc QA, summarization, few-shot learning, code completion, and synthetic tasks. Its balanced coverage of different task types makes it the most commonly reported accuracy metric across compression papers (KVQuant, GEAR, KIVI, MiniCache, RazorAttention, and many others all report LongBench accuracy). However, the original tasks have a maximum length of approximately 14k tokens 鈥?well below the 128k+ regime that motivates compression.\\n\\n**RULER** [2404.06654] addresses this gap by providing synthetic tasks specifically designed for extended context evaluation, extending the Needle-in-a-Haystack (NIAH) paradigm with multi-hop tracing (MH), variable tracking (VT), and common word extraction (CWE) tasks at lengths from 4k to 128k tokens. Nearly all compression methods now report RULER performance, making it the leading benchmark for assessing retrieval accuracy under compression.\\n\\nAdditional benchmarks include **L-Eval** [2307.11088] (20 sub-tasks, 508 documents, LLM-as-judge evaluation), **LV-Eval** [2402.05136] (5 length levels up to 256k with confusing fact insertion), **鈭濨ench** [2402.13718] (the first benchmark with average data length >100k tokens), and **BAMBOO** [2309.13345] (multi-task with 5 length levels).\\n\\n### 7.2 Emerging Evaluation Concerns\\n\\nA critical evaluation of evaluation practices comes from **Rethinking KV Cache Compression Techniques for Large Language Model Serving** [2503.24000], which identifies several systematic issues:\\n\\n1. **Output-length inflation**: When compressed models produce shorter outputs than full-cache baselines, accuracy comparisons are confounded 鈥?shorter outputs are penalized by metrics like F1 that reward verbosity.\\n2. **Serving gap**: Most compression papers report accuracy on LongBench but evaluate neither end-to-end serving latency nor memory bandwidth utilization under actual serving conditions.\\n3. **Implementation maturity**: Many compression implementations are not optimized for production serving stacks (PagedAttention, TensorRT-LLM), meaning reported memory savings may not translate to wall-clock improvements.\\n\\n**Accuracy Is Not All You Need** [2407.09141] proposes complementary metrics: KL divergence between compressed and full-cache output distributions, and flip-rate (the fraction of generated tokens that differ between compressed and full-cache inference). These metrics reveal that two methods with identical accuracy can have very different output distributions 鈥?an important consideration for applications requiring output fidelity.\\n\\n### 7.3 Specialized Evaluation Frameworks\\n\\n**KV-CoRE** [2602.05929] is the first dedicated compressibility benchmark, quantifying the low-rank compressibility of KV caches across five domains and 16 languages using SVD-based analysis. This provides a task-agnostic measure of how compressible a given input's KV cache is, independent of any specific compression method. **LOOM-Scope** [2507.04723] standardizes evaluation settings across diverse long-context benchmarks, providing a unified evaluation pipeline that reduces methodological variability across papers.\\n\\n**MiniLongBench** [2505.19959] prunes LongBench to 237 samples while maintaining 0.97 rank correlation with the full benchmark, reducing evaluation cost to 4.5% 鈥?an important practical contribution given that comprehensive compression evaluation is computationally expensive.\\n\\n### 7.4 Evaluation Challenges\\n\\nThree challenges dominate the evaluation landscape. First, **distribution shift** between benchmarks (primarily synthetic NIAH and multi-task QA) and real-world deployment (multi-turn dialogue, agentic reasoning, code analysis) means that benchmark performance may not predict production behavior. Second, the **reliability of NIAH** as a compression metric is under scrutiny 鈥?the single-needle variant is easily gamed, and multi-needle versions (RULER's MH) are still being adopted. Third, **multilingual generalization** remains underevaluated; ONERULER [2503.01996] extends RULER to 26 languages and finds substantial variability, suggesting that compression methods optimized on English may not transfer.\\n\\n### 7.5 The Leaderboard Gap\\n\\nNo standardized KV compression leaderboard exists as of mid-2026. Tools like **Evalica** [2412.11314] and **BenchBench** [2407.13696] provide infrastructure for reproducible comparisons, but the community has not converged on an agreed evaluation protocol. The KVpress leaderboard (associated with the KVpress repository) is a community effort, but it is not yet as widely adopted as, for example, the LLM-perf leaderboards. Establishing a KV compression leaderboard with standardized evaluation settings is a pressing community need.\\n\\n**Key references**: [2308.14508, 2404.06654, 2307.11088, 2402.05136, 2402.13718, 2309.13345, 2406.17588, 2505.19959, 2503.01996, 2510.17725, 2602.05929, 2409.16191, 2501.03200, 2507.04723, 2503.24000, 2406.14171, 2407.09141, 2407.13696, 2412.11314, 2402.14116, 1712.07040, 2112.08608]\\n\\n---\\n\\n## Section 8: System Integration and Inference Serving\\n\\n**Subtitle**: Deploying KV cache compression in production 鈥?serving systems, disaggregated architectures, and hardware co-design\\n\\n### 8.1 Compression in Serving Frameworks\\n\\nThe most widely deployed serving framework for LLMs, **vLLM** [2309.06180], implements PagedAttention 鈥?a page-based KV cache management system that avoids fragmentation and enables dynamic memory allocation. KV-Compress [2410.00161] was designed specifically for PagedAttention, implementing variable compression rates per head within the existing page table framework. **TensorRT-LLM** and **SGLang** provide their own KV cache management and are increasingly targeted by compression method implementations.\\n\\nA critical observation from the serving perspective is the **prefill-decode asymmetry**: the prefill phase is compute-bound (processing all input tokens in parallel), while the decode phase is memory-bound (generating one token at a time, dominated by KV cache reads). Compression disproportionately benefits the decode phase by reducing memory bandwidth 鈥?making decode latency the primary metric for evaluating compression's serving impact.\\n\\n### 8.2 Disaggregated Serving\\n\\nDisaggregated architectures separate prefill and decode across different GPU nodes, requiring the KV cache to be transferred between nodes. This introduces new optimization targets: compression reduces transfer time and bandwidth. **SplitZip** [2605.01708] provides ultra-fast lossless compression for KV cache transfer in disaggregated serving, achieving 613 GB/s compression throughput and 1.32脳 end-to-end speedup. **KVServe** [2605.13734] uses Bayesian profiling to automatically search for the optimal compression strategy for each request, achieving up to 32.8脳 TTFT reduction in disaggregated settings. **CacheGen** [2310.07240] encodes KV cache values for streaming across the network, evaluating compression-delay trade-offs and achieving 3.5鈥?.3脳 reduction.\\n\\n### 8.3 Multi-Tier Storage\\n\\nBeyond DRAM, several systems exploit tiered storage hierarchies. **AdaptCache** [2509.00105] manages KV cache across DRAM/SSD tiers with native compression, achieving 1.43鈥?.4脳 delay savings over single-tier baselines. **LayerKV** [2410.00428] implements layer-wise KV cache management, placing early-layer cache on fast memory and later layers on slower memory, exploiting the observation that deeper layers tolerate higher latency 鈥?achieving 69脳 TTFT improvement.\\n\\n### 8.4 Hardware-Software Co-Design\\n\\nFull-stack co-design approaches integrate compression algorithms with hardware capabilities. **Titanus** [2505.17787] combines on-the-fly pruning and quantization in a software-hardware co-design that achieves 159.9脳 energy efficiency improvement through cascade pruning-quantization pipelines. **Mustafar** introduces a bitmap sparse format for KV caches that reduces memory traffic through selective storage of non-zero entries.\\n\\n**HCAttention** implements heterogeneous GPU-CPU KV cache management, placing frequently accessed entries on GPU memory and infrequently accessed entries on CPU memory, with a learned predictor for access patterns.\\n\\n### 8.5 Multi-Tenant KV Reuse\\n\\nIn serving scenarios with multiple requests, opportunities exist for cross-request KV reuse. **KVShare** [2503.16525] identifies shared prefixes across requests and reuses KV cache entries, combined with dual-stage high-deviation detection to handle diverging continuations, achieving 9.39脳 average TTFT reduction. **Joint Encoding** [2601.03067] fuses similar cache blocks across requests, identifying semantic similarity beyond exact prefix matching and achieving 4.38脳 compression.\\n\\n### 8.6 Speculative Decoding and KV Cache\\n\\nSpeculative decoding creates a natural synergy with KV compression: compressed KV caches can serve as draft models. **QuantSpec** [2502.10424] uses a 4-bit quantized KV cache (and weights) as the draft model while retaining the full-precision target model, achieving >90% acceptance rate and 2.5脳 end-to-end speedup. **SpeCache** [2503.16163] combines CPU offload with speculative prefetching and low-bit importance scoring, achieving 10脳 effective compression. **Cacheback** [2511.21699] is a particularly elegant approach: it reuses the KV cache _itself_ as the draft model through LRU n-gram cache tables, requiring no separate draft model.\\n\\n### 8.7 The Algorithm-to-Serving Gap\\n\\nA persistent theme across serving research is the gap between algorithmic compression methods and their serving implementations. Many methods report impressive compression ratios on LongBench accuracy but do not provide efficient implementations in production serving frameworks. Adapting a compression method to PagedAttention, for example, may require custom CUDA kernels for non-uniform page sizes or variable compression schemas. This \\\"last mile\\\" problem is a significant barrier to adoption.\\n\\n### 8.8 Open Problems\\n\\nMaking compression methods PagedAttention-compatible without custom kernel modifications is a pressing engineering challenge. The interaction between compression and continuous batching (where requests are added and removed dynamically) is underexplored 鈥?most evaluations assume static batch configurations. Whether quantization, eviction, or hybrid methods can maintain accuracy at 10M+ token contexts remains unknown.\\n\\n2. **Zero-error lossless compression**: VeriCache [2605.17613] provides a blueprint for lossless inference with lossy compression through verification. Extending this to speculative decoding (where the compressed KV serves as a draft) could yield practical lossless compression at near-lossless efficiency.\\n\\n3. **Unified theoretical framework**: Current theory (Compression Barriers, KV-Direct, CapKV) provides partial views. A unified rate-distortion framework connecting quantization, eviction, and merging under a common objective would inform method design.\\n\\n4. **Distribution-shift robustness**: Methods calibrated on one task distribution fail on others (Section 3.7). Developing methods that adapt online to distribution shift 鈥?or are provably robust to it 鈥?is critical for production deployment.\\n\\n5. **Architectural generalization**: The retrieval head phenomenon (Section 4) has been studied primarily in LLaMA models.
