## Method Description

This paper presents a fusion architecture combining BERT (for text understanding) and GCN (for graph structure understanding) for context-aware citation recommendation. Given a query paper with its full text, the BERT encoder produces contextualized representations of the paper's content, particularly focusing on the sections where citations would be needed. The GCN encoder processes the citation graph to produce structural paper embeddings. The two representations are fused via concatenation or attention, producing a joint embedding that captures both what the paper is about (semantic) and where it sits in the research landscape (structural).

The model is trained to recommend citations for a given query paper by ranking candidate papers based on the similarity of their joint embeddings. The context-awareness comes from the fact that different sections of the query paper (introduction, related work, methodology) may require different types of citations, and the BERT encoder captures these section-level nuances. The paper reports a +28% MAP improvement over content-only or structure-only baselines.

## Category Rationale

This paper combines BERT-based text encoding with GCN-based graph encoding for citation recommendation. The citation graph is co-equal with text content as an input signal, making it a hybrid graph_enhanced_retrieval method. The GCN provides structural awareness that complements semantic understanding.

## Limitations

- Pre-dates modern LLMs (GPT-4, etc.); BERT encoder may not match current capabilities
- Fusion mechanism may not generalize across all citation recommendation scenarios
- Requires full text of query paper for context-aware recommendation
- Does not address multi-hop citation expansion or survey generation
